How the ratings are built

Backtested on every D1 game from 2011-12 through 2025-26, each predicted only from games before that day.

Each team gets an offensive and defensive rating in points per 100 possessions, adjusted for opponent and home court, fit jointly across all D1 games. The ELO number is that rating on an ELO scale, calibrated so ELO gaps map to actual win rates.

Preseason, every returning player and transfer gets a projected minutes share and production, learned from 2012–2026, including how both change when a player moves up or down a level and where he ranks among his new teammates. Team talent is those projections added up over 200 minutes, combined with last season's rating and the program's rating three to five seasons back.

Freshmen come from the 247Sports composite (top 300 per class). Coach +/− is how far a coach's past teams finished above or below what their talent alone predicted, in points per 100 against a schedule-adjusted rating, at any school, recent seasons weighted more and shrunk toward zero when the history is short. It counts most when a coach takes over a new program.

Transfer portal, 2026-27

Every D1-to-D1 transfer who played 100+ minutes last season, projected before the season. Portal tax = what the move itself costs: how his team impact projects at the new school vs. if he had stayed, from the size of the jump in team strength, his role and how good he was. Match grade = what this particular landing spot adds or takes away on top of that, from opportunity there (open minutes, at his position too; better players already on the roster; incoming top-100 recruits). Team impact = points per 100 possessions he adds to his team (box value × minutes share × 5). This season fills in from the weekly player update; "vs projection" appears once he has 3+ games and 60+ minutes. Projections, not betting advice.

How it works

Tested on every season from 2020-21 to 2025-26 with that season held out. The portal tax cut the error in projecting a transfer's team impact by about 10% vs. treating him like a returner. Opportunity at the new school added a smaller but consistent gain (better in all six held-out seasons): A-graded landing spots beat the tax-only projection by +0.20 pts / 100 on average, F-graded ones missed it by −0.29. Things that sound like "fit" but did not predict anything out of sample, so they are not in the grade: an offense-first player joining a team whose offense lags its defense (and the reverse), tempo match, following his coach, and a coaching change at the new school.

Limits: box value only (no lineup-based impact before 2025-26); D1-to-D1 moves only (no JUCO, D2 or international); last season's numbers aren't adjusted for opponents, which is exactly why moving up costs more. Best Match (ranking every school for a player who enters the portal) runs on the same engine and is planned for the offseason portal window.

Coach fingerprints

Every 2026-27 head coach's style from his D1 head-coaching seasons since 2010-11, as a percentile among coaches with a D1 history (higher = more of it; ball security: higher = fewer turnovers). Seasons with few games count less, so a coach with one or two years sits closer to the middle. Style travels with the coach: when coaches change schools, about 60% of their pace and of how many threes their defense allows shows up at the new school in year one, far more than the new school's own history. Development (pts / 100 his returning players add vs. an identical returner elsewhere) is a weak signal: it repeats only faintly from season to season, so only long-tenured coaches move far from zero. None of this improves game predictions (tested); it's here to describe teams.

PnP Resume ranking, 2026-27

Our power ratings answer who's best. This ranks who has earned it: only wins and losses count, not margin. Wins above bubble = wins minus what a bubble team (our No. 45) would be expected to win against the same schedule at the same sites (70% of the ranking). Colley = the BCS-era computer ranking: wins and losses adjusted for who you played, nothing from our model (30%). Head-to-head: the winner of a season series ranks directly above the loser when the two are close (no more losses, scores within 0.4 SD, a move of 6 spots or fewer). Strength of record = the chance a bubble team, or a top-4 team, would match this record or better against the same schedule (lower = harder to earn). Q1 = Quadrant 1 record (NET-style cut-offs, using our ranks). The PnP bracket on the Bracketology tab is picked from this ranking.
